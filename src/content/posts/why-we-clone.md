---
title: Why we teach founders by cloning products
description: A clone has a known answer. That one property makes it the fastest way for a founder to learn real software engineering.
pubDate: 2026-08-12
---

Most people learn to code on projects nobody would use: a to-do list, a weather app, a calculator. They're small enough to finish, but they never teach the hard part, which is how a real product's pieces fit together.

The other option is to build your own idea straight away. Founders love this, and it almost always stalls. You're learning to code and deciding what to build at the same time, so every bug turns into a product debate.

## A known answer

A clone fixes both problems. Amazon and Instagram are big enough to need every part of a modern stack, from mobile apps to payments to machine learning. And everyone on your team already knows how they should behave.

When your cart forgets an item, nobody argues about whether that's a feature. You open Amazon, see what it does, and go back to fixing your code. The product decisions are made for you, so all your attention goes on engineering.

## Why a team

A clone of Amazon is too big for one person in nine weeks, and that's deliberate. Each of the four roles owns one part and depends on the others. Frontend waits on an API. The API waits on a schema. Everything waits on a deploy.

Learning to hand work to each other, to agree an API contract and review each other's pull requests, is what makes this feel like a startup instead of a course.

## Then your own

In weeks 13 to 15, the same team points the same stack at one teammate's startup. By then, you're not learning to code and designing a product at once. You're just building, and it shows in what teams ship at demo day.
